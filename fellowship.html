CRITERION 1

Responsibility

Evidence of significant personal accountability for engineering, construction, commercial, safety and program decisions.

Professional Context

As Construction Manager at the Perkoa Zinc Mine in Burkina Faso, I was responsible for coordinating the delivery of critical Tailings Storage Facility infrastructure required to maintain mining operations. My responsibilities included construction planning, procurement, contractor management, quality assurance, risk management, safety coordination, program control and communication with the client and Engineer of Record.

The urgent TSF II Phase I and II berm-raising project provided approximately nine months of additional storage capacity. The work was completed in approximately twenty days at a cost of about USD 400,000, helping prevent an estimated eight-month interruption to mining operations.

CRITERION 2

Leadership

Evidence of leading multidisciplinary teams, establishing clarity, managing uncertainty and aligning stakeholders behind shared outcomes.

Professional Context

Effective delivery at the Perkoa Zinc Mine required more than technical construction management. I coordinated contractors, survey personnel, quality teams, procurement specialists, operations staff and engineering representatives whose priorities and responsibilities were different.

I created clear reporting and communication arrangements, coordinated daily priorities, managed interfaces between teams and maintained focus on safety, quality and operational continuity. During the COVID-19 period, I also coordinated revised working arrangements, workforce rotation and local contractor support while maintaining project progress and avoiding recorded infections within the project team.

CRITERION 3

Insight & Experience

Evidence of applying engineering judgment, transferring knowledge between sectors and developing practical solutions to complex problems.

Professional Context

At the Ovacık Wind Power Plant in Türkiye, I managed civil works, logistics and turbine erection activities for eight Nordex N131 wind turbines. Each turbine blade was approximately 65.5 metres long, and transportation through mountainous terrain involved narrow curves, overhead constraints and restricted road geometry.

A blade-lifter system was used to elevate the blades to an angle of up to approximately 45 degrees, enabling the convoy to negotiate difficult sections while reducing the need for extensive road widening and associated environmental disturbance. The practical engineering solution received national media coverage.
